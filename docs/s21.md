# How the app computes S21

What the |S21| and phase traces are, from the DAC to the screen, and how they compare with a Keysight PNA-X. QICK details are checked against the installed library (0.2.442) and the board's firmware configuration (`tests/fixtures/soccfg_rfsoc4x2.json`); PNA-X facts are in [research/pna-x.md](research/pna-x.md).

## Short version

The trace is the complex amplitude of the tone that reaches the ADC, measured by a digital receiver locked to the generator, and divided by the gain setting:

  S21 = (I + jQ) / gain  (conjugated on the lower sideband; on the I/Q return path, Y_I + j·Y_Q from two readouts)

Its **shape** is the chip's: resonance frequency, linewidth, Q, depth relative to the background, phase turn. Its **absolute level and phase offset** are those of the whole chain (DAC, mixers, cables, cryostat lines, amplifiers, ADC), so the dB value is relative, not calibrated.

## 1. Generator

For each point, the tProc plays a tone on a DAC (`axis_signal_gen_v6`, a DDS at 9830.4 MSa/s):

  v(t) = gain · V_FS · cos(2π f t + φ)

- `gain` is the **Gain** setting, 0–1, a fraction of the DAC's full scale V_FS.
- `f` is the IF. With an LO, RF = LO + IF (upper sideband) or LO − IF (lower).
- On the I/Q output the same tone goes to two DACs, 90° apart, with the I/Q calibration.

QICK calls (in `iq.py`): `declare_gen`, `add_pulse(..., ro_ch=…)` (via `add_tone`), `pulse` (via `play_tone`).

## 2. Readout: from a real signal to I + jQ

The ADC samples the returned signal at 4423.68 MSa/s. It is **real**: one number per sample, AC-coupled.

  x[n] = A · cos(2π f t_n + θ)

`A` and `θ` hold everything the signal went through. Each QICK readout (`axis_dyn_readout_v1`) then works like a digital PNA-X receiver:

1. **Reference oscillator.** A DDS at the frequency set by `add_readoutconfig(freq=f, gen_ch=…)` makes e^{−j2πft} (cos and −sin).
2. **Product** (`outsel="product"`, the default; "the input is real, the output is complex"):

   x·e^{−j2πft} = (A/2)·e^{jθ} + (A/2)·e^{−j(4πft+θ)}

   The first term is at DC and holds amplitude and phase. The second spins at 2f.
3. **Rate reduction** by 8, to 552.96 MSa/s.
4. **Accumulation** over the readout window (`declare_readout(length=…)`, opened by `trigger(ros=[…])`): the averaging buffer adds I and Q over the window's N samples.

**Frequency matching.** `ro_ch` on the pulse and `gen_ch` on the readout round both frequencies to one common grid (≈ 20.6 Hz on this board), so generator and readout DDS are equal to the last bit. Both run from the same clock, so θ is repeatable from run to run, which is why the phase means something.

### The window is the IF filter

Adding up a window of T seconds and dividing by N is a boxcar filter. Around the readout frequency its response is

  |H(Δf)| = |sin(π Δf T) / (π Δf T)|

- **Noise bandwidth ≈ 1/T.** This is the PNA-X's IF bandwidth: a 10 µs window ≈ 100 kHz, 100 µs ≈ 10 kHz. The app keeps the window as the setting.
- **Nulls at Δf = k/T.** Sidelobes are weak (first one −13 dB, then −6 dB per octave), so a nearby spur or image leaks more than through a PNA-X's shaped IF filter.
- **The 2f term** is suppressed by about 1/(π·2f·T): −65 dB at f = 200 MHz, T = 1.5 µs; only −30 dB at f = 10 MHz, T = 0.5 µs. Keep the IF at a few tens of MHz or more with short windows.

## 3. From the board to S21

| Step | Code | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Collect the sums | `QickBoard._acquire_round` (our bounded copy of QICK's `finish_round`: `start_readout`, `poll_data`, `cleanup_round`) | Raw I and Q sums per shot |
| Average shots, normalize | QICK `_process_accumulated` → `_average_buf` | Divided by N (and the readout's I/Q offset, 0 here): (A/2)·e^{jθ} in ADC units |
| Average rounds | QICK `_summarize_accumulated` (CW Time uses each round alone) | Running average |
| Complex number | `board.py`: `m[0].dot([1, 1j])` | I + jQ |
| To S21 | `measurements/base.py`: `received_s21` | I/Q return: Y_I + j·Y_Q with the RX calibration; real return: conjugated on the lower sideband; both divided by the readout tone's gain (as QICK rounded it) |
| Traces | each measurement | 20·log10\|S21\|, and the phase (resonator sweep: multiplied by e^{+j2π·f·τ} to remove the electrical delay τ, stored in the setup from a wide sweep or else fitted per sweep, then a constant offset removed; wrapped to ±180°) |

The saved `s21` array is the complex S21 before the delay removal.

## 4. Why divide by the gain

On a PNA-X, S21 = b2/a1:

- **a1** is the wave going into the device at port 1. A coupler sends a copy to the reference receiver R1.
- **b2** is the wave leaving the device at port 2, measured by the test receiver B.

Each receiver gives a complex phasor like our I + jQ. Their ratio cancels the source's amplitude and phase, so S21 does not change with source power.

We have no reference receiver, but we do not need one for the amplitude: the DAC is the source and its amplitude is set digitally. On the bench loopback the output follows the gain exactly (−6.02 dB for half, −19.93 dB for a tenth). Dividing by the gain therefore plays the role of a1:

- Traces at different gains are directly comparable.
- A real power dependence of the chip (shift with photon number, punch-out, compression) shows up as a change in S21, as in a PNA-X power sweep.
- Low gains look noisier: dividing does not change the signal-to-noise ratio.
- Gain 0 gives gaps.

## 5. The three wiring cases

The LO's own phase cancels in every case, because the same LO converts up and down. Only a difference in the two LO cable paths remains.

### Real mixer out, real mixer in (fluxonium plan)

A real up-converter sends **both** LO + IF and LO − IF to the chip, equally strong. A real down-converter folds both back onto the same IF:

  s21 ∝ H(LO+IF) + H*(LO−IF)

- The two terms keep a fixed relative phase, set by the LO and the cable lengths.
- The resonance sits on a background of similar size from the other sideband. A full notch can show as only about 6 dB deep, or look asymmetric, and the background changes with the LO.
- The image term *adds* to the signal, so it also biases resonator fits, whose background model *multiplies*.
- Fixes: a band-pass filter after the up-converter that blocks LO − IF, or an I/Q mixer on the output or on the return.

### I/Q mixer out, real mixer in (transmon setup)

- The I/Q output is nearly single-sideband: the image is 25–30 dB down uncalibrated, lower after I/Q calibration.
- Result: H(RF) plus a small ε·H*(image), about ±0.1–0.3 dB when calibrated.

### I/Q mixer in, two ADCs (I/Q return path)

The mixer's I and Q outputs form a complex baseband z = I + jQ. RF above the LO appears at +IF and RF below it at −IF, so the receiver can tell the sidebands apart.

Each QICK readout takes **one real input**. So the I wire and the Q wire each go to their own readout, at the same frequency, triggered together:

- Y_I = ⟨I·e^{−jωt}⟩ and Y_Q = ⟨Q·e^{−jωt}⟩.
- Averaging is linear, so ⟨z·e^{−jωt}⟩ = Y_I + j·Y_Q exactly. The FPGA has already computed the four real products (I·cos, I·sin, Q·cos, Q·sin); the two sums are done in software.
- **Upper sideband: S21 ∝ Y_I + j·Y_Q.** The lower sideband cancels.
- **Lower sideband: S21 ∝ conj(Y_I − j·Y_Q).**

The rejection is only as good as the balance between the two paths: the mixer's I/Q phase and gain, the I and Q cables (about 48 ps per cm of difference), and the ADC paths. The **RX I/Q calibration** measures them: an ideal mixer gives Y_Q = −j·Y_I (upper sideband; +j·Y_I on the lower), so the ratio r = Y_Q/(∓j·Y_I) across a band is fitted with g·e^{j(φ + 2π·f·τ)} (gain ratio, phase offset, delay). Everything before the mixer multiplies I and Q alike and cancels in r, so this works with the chip in place. `received_s21` then divides Y_Q by g·e^{j(φ + 2π·f·τ)} before combining. A fixed phase between the two readouts' oscillators is absorbed into φ; one that changed from run to run would not be, which `scripts/hardware_two_readouts.py` checks.

Homodyne detection (LO = RF, then average the DC I and Q) is not possible here, because the 4x2's ADC inputs are AC-coupled.

### When I/Q down-conversion is worth it

- It removes the image band's noise, about **3 dB better SNR** (half the averaging time).
- It rejects signals that fall on the image frequency.
- It undoes the double-sideband background of a real up-converter.
- It costs both general-purpose readouts, one more cable and a receive calibration.

A band-pass filter in front of a real down-converter that blocks the image band gives most of the noise and image benefit with one ADC.

## 6. Lower sideband

With RF = LO − IF, the mixer delivers the chip's response with its phase mirrored: the IF carries −∠S21. `received_s21` conjugates S21 on the lower sideband, so the phase, and the direction the resonance circle turns, are the same on either sideband. |S21| is unaffected.

## 7. What the numbers mean, and what they don't

| Quantity | Meaningful? |
|---|---|
| Resonance frequency, linewidth, Q (QL, Qi, Qc from a circle fit) | Yes: they come from the shape, and a circle fit removes a smooth background (amplitude, phase, delay, mismatch). |
| Depth of the dip relative to the background | Yes. |
| Changes with power (gain divided out), flux, time | Yes. |
| Absolute dB level | No: it includes the whole chain. It reads as "dB (relative)". For absolute power use **Units → Absolute** (Scale menu): dBm at the ADC input, from the board calibration (`power.py`). |
| Absolute phase | No: a fixed offset from the chain and the readout reference. |

### Agreement with a PNA-X

Fitted Q values should agree with a PNA-X's within the fit uncertainties, if both measure under the same conditions:

- **Same power at the chip.** Qi depends on photon number. Our gain is not calibrated in dBm; that needs a power calibration of the output (power at the cryostat input against gain).
- **Steady state.** Use **CW** mode with a settling time of 5–10 × 1/κ per point. Short pulses distort the lineshape, as does a PNA-X sweeping too fast.
- **Single sideband.** The real/real double-sideband background biases Qc and Qi.
- **Same span, points and SNR.** About 10 or more points within the linewidth, a span of 5–10 linewidths.
- **Same frequency reference** (10 MHz shared) if f₀ must match to the kHz.

### Normalization (not built)

A PNA-X can divide by a stored reference: Data/Memory, or a Thru Response calibration. Dividing by a reference without the resonance would leave only the chip, but inside a cryostat there is no thru, so the reference could only be partial:

- a loopback at the cryostat top (removes the room-temperature electronics);
- the chip above its critical temperature during cooldown;
- the resonance moved away by flux.

Since the quantities that matter come from the resonance's shape, the app does not normalize. The **Scale** menu (fixed reference level and scale) lines up traces from different runs on screen.
